## Premise

The world is **Vesper**, a tidally locked planet where one side always faces its sun and the other never does. Life exists only in the twilight band between them, where it is neither burned nor frozen. The two monitors show one continuous slice of that band, running from the scorched day side to the black night side.

The question Vesper raises is simple: life is whatever keeps itself going by feeding on a gradient. Here the gradient is visible as light fading into dark, and every creature is a temporary pattern that holds out against it. Nothing on screen is scripted. Every body, behavior, alliance and extinction comes out of the simulation, and the world never repeats.

Three rules hold everywhere:

- **Nothing is free.** Matter is conserved. Every creature is built from nutrients that something else gave up, and gives them back when it dies.
- **Nothing is scripted.** The sim provides physics, chemistry, bodies and minds. Hunting, cooperation, war, mourning-like behavior and culture have to emerge, or they don't happen.
- **Nothing is cute.** No faces, no big eyes, no mascots. Creatures should feel like real wildlife from somewhere else: strange, beautiful, and indifferent to you.

## The world

The view is a side-on slice through the twilight band, 7680 by 2160 pixels across both monitors. It reads like a living diorama with depth layers: far ridges in haze, a mid layer where most life happens, and a near layer of ground and roots. The sun sits low and huge on the day edge and never moves.

- **Day edge.** Amber light, bleached mineral flats, heat shimmer. Rich in energy but lethal to soft bodies for long.
- **Twilight middle.** The habitable heart. Violet and teal light, dense floating life, the most competition.
- **Night edge.** Near-black, cold, lit only by bioluminescence and mineral vents. Little energy, strange specialists.

The air is thick enough to swim in, like a shallow sea. Many creatures float or swim through it, others crawl the ground or root in it. A permanent wind runs night-ward high up and day-ward near the ground, so creatures can ride currents in both directions.

**The land moves too.** Ice caps the night side. Where it meets the twilight it melts into rivers that run day-ward across the whole view, carving channels, pooling in lakes, and boiling off as mist near the sun. Volcanoes and vent fields smoke, glow and sometimes erupt, sending lava down slopes and ash into the air. Rain falls in bands where warm and cold air meet, with lightning in the heaviest storms. Rivers shift course over days, so the map itself slowly changes.

**Alien plants.** Rooted life is a full kingdom of its own, evolving under the same rules as the creatures. Light-film fans track the sun, vent feeders crust around hot springs, river-bank thickets drink from the water, and some plants drift free as floating mats. They sway in the wind, close up in storms, bloom, seed, and die back in famine. Plants also evolve defenses like spines, toxins and traps.

**Seasons come from wobble.** Vesper rocks slightly in its orbit, so the twilight band slides back and forth over a cycle of several real days. Habitats shrink and grow, which forces migrations, famines and new frontiers. **Weather** adds smaller shocks: dust storms off the day side, cold falls from the night side, and vent eruptions that seed the ground with minerals.

## Biology

A genome is a growth program, not a blueprint. It tells a single seed cell how to divide, branch and specialize as it grows, so body shape comes from development. A small mutation can add a limb pair, change symmetry, or do nothing at all, just as in real evolution.

Bodies are built from linked segments of tissue. Each tissue has a job and a cost:

| Tissue | What it does | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Frame | Shape and attachment points | Low |
| Muscle | Movement, grip, strikes | Energy to use |
| Light film | Feeds on sunlight | Fragile, needs light |
| Gut | Digests food, sets diet | Upkeep |
| Float bladder | Buoyancy in the thick air | Volume |
| Senses | Light, scent, pressure waves | Brain load |
| Glow cells | Signals and lures | Energy per flash |
| Armor and spines | Defense and weapons | Heavy, slow |
| Store | Fat for famine | Weight |
| Brood | Eggs, buds or spores | Large |

**Two currencies run everything.** Energy comes from light, vent chemistry, or eating. Matter is a fixed amount of nutrient in the whole world. A creature can't grow bigger than the matter it has eaten.

**Growth and age.** Creatures hatch small and grow as they eat, and their bodies change shape as they mature. Damage builds over time, and how fast they age is itself heritable. Some lineages will evolve to live fast and die young, others to live long and slow.

**Reproduction is evolvable.** Lineages can bud clones, scatter spores, lay eggs, or mate and mix genomes. Mating needs courtship and mutual choice, so mate preferences can split one species into two. Parents decide how much to invest, from thousands of untended spores to one offspring guarded for its whole youth.

**Death is a stage, not an ending.** A corpse keeps its shape and slowly breaks down. Scavengers strip it and decomposers colonize it. Its matter returns to the ground, where rooted life takes it up again.

## Minds

Every creature has a real neural network, grown from its genome alongside its body. Brains range from a dozen neurons in a drifting grazer to a few hundred in a social hunter. More neurons cost more energy, so intelligence only spreads where it pays.

**Learning happens in two ways.** Evolution shapes the wiring a creature is born with. During its life, the connections then change through reward and pain signals, the same principle real nervous systems use. A hunter that gets stung learns to avoid that prey. A grazer that finds a rich patch learns to come back.

**Drives, not scripts.** Creatures feel hunger, pain, fear, fatigue, the pull to mate, and curiosity. Each drive is an internal signal the brain learns to satisfy. No behavior is hand-coded beyond basic reflexes like flinching from damage.

**Memory of others.** Smarter lineages can recognize individuals by their scent and glow pattern. They remember who shared food, who attacked them, and who is kin. That is the raw material for alliances, grudges and revenge.

**Copying and culture.** Creatures can learn by watching others, especially their elders. A useful trick, like a migration route or a way to crack armored prey, can spread through a group and outlive the one who found it. Different groups will drift into different traditions.

**Signals become language.** Glow cells let creatures flash patterns at each other, and nothing assigns those patterns meaning. Meanings get learned and passed on, so dialects form and split between groups. You'll literally see them talk, and the chronicle will track which groups can still understand each other.

## Ecology and the dark side

Food webs are not designed. Producers, grazers, predators, parasites, scavengers and decomposers all have to evolve from the same starting stock. Vesper will be as harsh as the sim allows, and violence is shown fully, not cut away.

- **Predation is physical.** Bodies are real segmented structures, so predators tear pieces off. Prey can survive losing a limb and limp away leaking glowing fluid. A big kill draws a crowd of scavengers within minutes.
- **Lures and lies.** Hunters can evolve glow patterns that mimic food or a mating call, then strike whatever comes close. Prey can evolve to fake being toxic.
- **Parasites live inside.** Many creatures are translucent, so you can watch a parasite grow inside its host. Some will evolve to steer the host's brain, making it walk into predators or toward the next host. Some burst out.
- **Disease is alive too.** Pathogens are their own evolving lineages that spread by contact. Plagues sweep through crowded colonies, and survivors pass on resistance.
- **Famine and the slide.** When the twilight band slides, whole habitats fail. Populations starve, turn on each other, or flee into regions held by others.
- **Cruelty that pays.** Cannibalism, killing a rival's young, and laying eggs in another species' brood can all evolve if they work.
- **Extinction is permanent.** When the last of a lineage dies, it is gone. The chronicle keeps its record.

The beautiful side gets the same honesty. Parents can evolve to give their own bodies as their young's first meal. Partners can evolve symbiosis, one providing light and the other protection. Creatures can heal, and some will die defending kin.

## Societies and culture

Social life grows out of the same pressures that real evolutionary theory describes. The sim gives creatures a small set of social actions: follow, signal, share food, groom or heal, attack, guard, and mate. What they build from those is up to them.

- **Kinship first.** Helping relatives spreads your own genes, so families and clans should appear early. In extreme cases, sterile helpers and a single breeder can evolve, like ants or naked mole rats.
- **Reciprocity and cheaters.** Unrelated creatures can cooperate if they remember who paid back. That invites cheaters, and in turn punishment of cheaters.
- **Hierarchy.** Dominance ranks can form inside groups, with fights over food and mates. Leaders can be overthrown.
- **Territory and war.** Groups can claim rich ground and defend it. When the band slides and land runs short, raids and wars between groups follow, including wiping out a rival group.
- **Tradition.** Learned habits pass from elders to the young. Groups develop their own migration routes, hunting methods, and dialects of light.
- **Sacrifice.** Creatures can evolve to die for kin, to guard a nest to the end, or to stay beside a dying partner.

When a group splits, its culture splits with it. Over time you can watch two branches of one family stop understanding each other and become rivals.

## Visual language

The look is a natural-history field guide brought to life, lit like deep-sea photography. Life has no single style. Like Earth, Vesper holds the full range from larval swimmers to web-building ambushers, heavy shaggy foragers, river swimmers, gliders, burrowers and parasites, each shaped by how it lives. Anatomy is believable and functional, but the details are never Earth's: different limb counts and joints, unfamiliar mouths, strange hides, shells and membranes. Every creature is drawn procedurally from its own genome, so the art style is a set of rules the renderer follows, not a library of sprites.

**Palette by zone.**

| Zone | Light | Accents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day edge | Burnt amber, rust, bone-white haze | Molten gold sun glare |
| Twilight | Deep violet, slate teal | Warm rim light on every body |
| Night edge | Indigo to true black | Cyan, magenta and acid-green glow |

**Materials.** Bodies are translucent, with light passing through thin tissue so you can see organs, eggs and parasites inside. Armor reads as matte mineral or lacquered shell. Color is vibrant and high contrast, never pastel: deep shadows, rich jewel tones in life and minerals, and glow cells as the brightest points of all.

**Atmosphere.** Thick haze gives depth between layers. Drifting particles, spores and decay flakes fall slowly through the light, like marine snow.

**Motion.** Everything is slow and weighty, as if underwater. Movement comes from muscles and physics, not canned animations. Violence is sudden against that calm, which makes it land.

**HDR.** On the HDR monitor, the sun, glow signals and vent eruptions go genuinely bright against deep blacks. The SDR monitor gets a matched tone-mapped version so the seam between screens is invisible.

**Desktop manners.** A low-contrast quiet zone sits behind the icon column so icons stay readable. Nothing flashes fast or strobes.

**Avoid.** One shared look for all life, spiky starburst or urchin forms, faces, eyes that read as cute, fur that reads as cuddly, smiles, pastel colors, cartoon outlines, Spore-style toy creatures, and the generic jellyfish screensaver look.
